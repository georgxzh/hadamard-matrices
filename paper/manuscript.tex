\begin{abstract}
We study binary lifts of a prescribed quadratic-character compression at
length $9p$. Established compression identities give an exact two-stage
formulation through length $3p$. We spell out translation gauges, a
sufficient projected set of autocorrelation equations, and a ternary
minority-layer parameterization retaining every cross term and layer carry.
Two independent exhaustive enumerators support a restricted census for
$p=5$: the domain consists of the 1,164 translation orbits of intermediate
pairs above the specified length-five pair: 704 have lifts and 460 are empty,
with exact multiplicities retained. This is not a classification of all Legendre
pairs of length 45 or of Hadamard equivalence classes. Bounded $p=5,7$
experiments compare exact enumeration with pseudo-Boolean (PB) encodings.
A certified spectral implementation reproduces the whole restricted census
and exhausts one saved $p=7$ intermediate branch with zero binary lifts,
independently confirmed using all nonredundant PAF shifts.
Order 668 motivates the longer-term problem, but none of these experiments
establish proximity to a solution; no $p=37$ search is performed.
Novelty is provisional: compression, character prescriptions, successive
lifting, symmetry reduction, and correlation matching have primary precedents.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and relation to primary literature}
Binary Legendre pairs yield Hadamard matrices of order $2(L+1)$ through a
standard two-core construction \cite{fletcher}. Thus $L=333=9\cdot37$ is one
possible route to order 668. This paper studies small exact validation
problems for that route. A lift satisfying compressed necessary conditions
need not exist. We make no claim that an order-668 matrix is close, that the
prescribed family must contain one, or that our timings extrapolate to $p=37$.

The contribution under investigation is a reproducible restricted census,
with explicit count conventions and independently checked computations,
together with concrete exact formulations. The following ledger distinguishes
mathematical provenance from what this repository has reproduced.

\paragraph{Literature access and remaining gaps.}
The full primary compression text and the relevant sections of the quaternary
and multiple-of-five papers were read. The ISSAC primary publisher text identifies the exact
prescription and successive-lifting algorithm. The publisher abstract and
metadata of the later $pq^2$ paper were checked, but its full algorithm and
tables have not been audited. Full comparisons with the low-complexity
spectral algorithm and decimation-counting
work remain incomplete. These are substantive novelty gaps. We do not claim
a first algorithm, first classification, or state-of-the-art speed.
The quaternary Gray map in \cite{lebedev} is distinct from the reflected
ternary Gray traversal used here; quaternary existence results do not settle
binary length 333.

Fletcher, Gysin, and Seberry already report 3,058 inequivalent length-45
pairs in Table 3 of \cite{fletcher}, identifying row exchange, independent
cyclic shifts and reversals, and common decimation. Our count uses a fixed
ordered compression and keeps those multiplicities. We now convert our subset
to their stated equivalence convention, obtaining 63 classes below. Their
underlying list has not been obtained, so a record-by-record crosswalk is
still missing. Section 2, Tables 1--2 of
\cite{mod5} include length 45 and the magnitude pattern $(1,3,3,3,3)$
used here, alongside $(1,1,1,3,5)$ and other spectral families. Our census
does not cover these other prescriptions. No conjectural balance, trace,
or linear-complexity filter is used to exclude a lift within our domain.
In particular, the trace/linear-complexity discard in Section 4 of
\cite{issac} invokes its Conjecture 3.5 and is not used here.
Sections 3.4--5 of \cite{lumsden} give primary precedents for
complementary-signature matching with all bucket combinations, multilevel
compression, recursive uncompression, and equivalence filtering. Our exact
integer PAF keys and phase-support filters are implementation choices within
this established framework. We have not benchmarked their implementation.
Bright et al.\ \cite[Sections 3.3 and 3.5]{brightgolay} already combine
partial-correlation conflicts with spectral filtering in a complex aperiodic
Golay search. Their FFT filter uses an explicit numerical tolerance and
precomputed contributions. Here the periodic binary PSD threshold is
certified using rational coefficient bounds and rectangles for partial
ternary assignments. This specialization is potentially useful, but its
priority remains unresolved; it is not a new PSD exclusion principle.

\section{Mathematical setup and exact compression}
Indices of a length-$L$ sequence lie in $\Z_L$. For a real sequence $x$, put
\[
 \PAF_x(s)=\sum_{i\in\Z_L}x_i x_{i+s},\qquad
 \DFT_x(t)=\sum_{j\in\Z_L}x_j e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}jt/L},\qquad
 \PSD_x(t)=|\DFT_x(t)|^2.
\]
A normalized binary Legendre pair is an ordered pair
$(x,y)\in\{\pm1\}^L\times\{\pm1\}^L$, with odd $L$, both row sums 1,
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lp}
 F(0)=2L,\quad F(s)=-2\ (s\ne0),\qquad
 F(s)=\PAF_x(s)+\PAF_y(s).
\end{equation}
Its nonzero-frequency PSD sum is $2L+2$; its zero-frequency sum is 2.
For negative supports $X,Y$, both sizes are $(L-1)/2$, and the combined
number of ordered support differences at each nonzero shift is
$\lambda=(L-3)/2$. Indeed,
$\PAF_x(s)=L-4|X|+4|X\cap(X-s)|$. These identities permit independent
integer PAF and SDS validation without floating-point tolerances.

For $L=md$ define compression $C_d(x)_j=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}x_{j+kd}$.
The standard identities \cite{compression} are
\begin{align}
 \PAF_{C_d(x)}(r)&=\sum_{h=0}^{m-1}\PAF_x(r+hd),\label{eq:compress}\\
 \DFT_{C_d(x)}(t)&=\DFT_x(mt),\label{eq:fourier}\\
 C_e(C_d(x))&=C_e(x)\qquad(e\mid d\mid L).\label{eq:associate}
\end{align}
For completeness, expanding the left side of \eqref{eq:compress} gives
all pairs of layers exactly once after grouping their difference modulo $m$.
In \eqref{eq:fourier}, the exponential is constant on each residue class
modulo $d$. Equation \eqref{eq:associate} follows by partitioning the same
index set twice. For a Legendre pair, the compressed PAF sum is therefore
$2L-2(m-1)$ at zero and $-2m$ elsewhere.

Take $L=9p$, $N=3p$, with odd prime $p$, and let $\chi_p$ be the quadratic
character, extended by $\chi_p(0)=0$. The prescribed length-$p$ pair is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prescribed}
 A_0=B_0=1,\qquad A_j=3\chi_p(j),\quad B_j=-3\chi_p(j)\quad(j\ne0).
\end{equation}
This is the $q=3$ instance of the established prescription in
\cite[Theorem 3.2]{issac}. Writing $A=e_0+3\chi_p$, $B=e_0-3\chi_p$
cancels the mixed terms. The character autocorrelation is $p-1$ at zero
and $-1$ elsewhere, yielding $18p-16$ and $-18$, respectively. Each row
sums to 1. This verifies compressed necessary conditions, not liftability;
the universal uncompression claim in \cite{issac} is conjectural.

An admissible intermediate pair $(C,D)$ has length $N$, entries
$\{-3,-1,1,3\}$, compressions $(A,B)$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intermediate}
 G(0)=6N-4,\qquad G(r)=-6\ (r\ne0),\quad
 G(r)=\PAF_C(r)+\PAF_D(r).
\end{equation}
Both row sums are 1. If $k_C,k_D$ count magnitude-one entries, then
$18N-8(k_C+k_D)=6N-4$, hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:active}
 k_C+k_D=(3N+1)/2=(9p+1)/2.
\end{equation}
For $p=5$, $(A,B)=((1,3,-3,-3,3),(1,-3,3,3,-3))$ and the active total is 23.

\section{Translation conventions and a projected PAF theorem}
At the first stage, independent translations of $C,D$ by multiples of $p$
preserve the prescribed pair and all PAFs. Anchor a residue whose prescribed
sum is $1$ or $-1$. Its three intermediate entries cannot all be equal,
so its rotation orbit has size three. Each pair orbit consequently has size
nine. The saved catalogue uses the lexicographically least entire row under
these rotations, separately for each row.

The PB implementation instead chooses the lexicographically least anchor
triple. To encode it, write its entries as $c_i=3-2n_i$ with
$n_i=u_i+2v_i\in\{0,1,2,3\}$. Maximizing $16n_0+4n_1+n_2$ among rotations
is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauge}
 15n_0-12n_1-3n_2\ge0,\qquad
 12n_0+3n_1-15n_2\ge0.
\end{equation}
There are two inequalities per row, four per pair, with no added variables.
Both conventions select one representative per orbit, but need not select
the same representative. Witnesses must be translated before comparing them.

For a fixed intermediate row, binary translations by $N$ preserve it.
An active residue has exactly one minority sign among three layers. Setting
the first active residue's minority layer to zero fixes this free action.
For the present row-sum-one branches, active residues always exist, giving
another factor nine for ordered binary pairs. This phase-zero gauge differs
from the binary PB lexicographic negative-bit gauge: the latter selects
minority layer 2 when the anchor compression is $+1$, and layer 0 when it
is $-1$. Controlled encoding comparisons align these choices explicitly.

\begin{theorem}[Sufficient projected shifts]\label{thm:projected}
Suppose binary rows of length $3N$ compress to an admissible intermediate
pair satisfying \eqref{eq:intermediate}. They form a Legendre pair if and
only if $F(s)=-2$ for every $1\le s<N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Necessity is immediate. For $1\le r<N$, compression gives
$F(r)+F(r+N)+F(r+2N)=-6$. Real PAF symmetry and periodicity give
$F(r+2N)=F(N-r)=-2$, so $F(r+N)=-2$. At zero, $F(0)=6N$ and
$G(0)=F(0)+F(N)+F(2N)=6N-4$. Symmetry gives $F(N)=F(2N)=-2$.
All shifts are covered, and compression preserves the row sums.
\end{proof}
This is a specialization of established compression theory, not a claim of
minimality or of novel general compression. Dropping equations is justified
only after checking the intermediate constants exactly.

\section{Ternary phases and exact cross terms}
For one intermediate row define $b_i=\operatorname{sgn}(C_i)$ and
$d_i=-2b_i$ when $|C_i|=1$, otherwise $d_i=0$. Associate a phase
$a_i\in\Z_3$ to each active residue. Every binary preimage is uniquely
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decode}
 x_{i+kN}=b_i+d_i\ind{k=a_i}\qquad(0\le i<N,\ k\in\Z_3).
\end{equation}
Inactive phases can be assigned arbitrarily because their coefficients vanish.
For a shift $s=r+\ell N$, $0\le r<N$, put
$j=(i+r)\bmod N$ and $h_i=\ell+\lfloor(i+r)/N\rfloor\pmod3$.
Expanding \eqref{eq:decode} and summing over layers gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase}
 \PAF_x(s)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}
 \left(3b_i b_j+d_i b_j+b_i d_j+
 d_i d_j\ind{a_j-a_i=h_i}\right).
\end{equation}
The carry in $h_i$ is essential. Diagonal terms at multiples of $N$ are
handled by the same formula. The derivation uses the fact that two minority
indicators coincide in exactly one layer precisely when their phase
difference equals $h_i$.

\paragraph{PB encoding.}
Use one-hot bits $X_{i,a}$ for phases and $D_{ij,h}$ for each unordered pair
of active residues and each difference $h\in\Z_3$. Add exactly-one equations
for each triple and nine implications per edge:
\[
 -X_{i,a}-X_{j,b}+D_{ij,b-a}\ge-1\qquad(a,b\in\Z_3).
\]
The selected endpoint phases force their unique difference bit. Conversely
every phase assignment extends uniquely to all difference bits. Substitute
these bits in \eqref{eq:phase}, with a fixed orientation and sign reversal
when an edge is reversed, and impose shifts $1,\ldots,N-1$. Coefficients of
the variable terms are multiples of four and can be divided by four along
with the integer target. Fixing the anchor phase removes translations.
This formulation is equivalent to binary lifting by
Theorem~\ref{thm:projected}. It is not a relaxation.

For $k_C,k_D$ active residues the encoding has $3(k_C+k_D)$ phase bits and
$3\{\binom{k_C}{2}+\binom{k_D}{2}\}$ difference bits. Cross terms between
any proposed partition of a row remain present. The interaction graph is
complete within each row; a small separator or square-root search bound
cannot be assumed.

\paragraph{Exact enumeration and incremental updates.}
With the anchor fixed, a row has $3^{k-1}$ assignments. Reflected ternary
Gray order changes one phase per step. For active positions $u<v$ of
distance $r$, its weight $d_ud_v$ contributes to projected coordinate $r$
if $a_v-a_u=0$, to coordinate $N-r$ if the difference is 2, and to neither
if it is 1. Removing and reinserting all incident-edge contributions updates
the entire PAF vector exactly. This costs $O(k)$ arithmetic operations per
transition, rather than eliminating the exponential enumeration.
Join the two row streams by complementary PAF vectors. Signature buckets
retain every multiplicity and, for the census, every matched binary pair.

\subsection{Sound propagation and a row-conditioned search}
For each ordered orientation $u<v$ of active positions, let
$e_{uv}=a_v-a_u\in\Z_3$. A finite domain $D_{uv}\subseteq\Z_3$ is
propagated against $e_{uv}+e_{vw}=e_{uw}$ on triangles. The implementation
tabulates exact supports for all $8^3$ triples of domain masks. It either
uses all triangles or only those incident with anchor 0. At singleton
domains the latter suffice: setting $a_0=0$ and $a_v=e_{0v}$ realizes every
edge, since $e_{uv}=e_{0v}-e_{0u}$.

After grouping each edge's contribution, a projected PAF equation has form
$\sum_e f_e(e)=T$. For current domains, set
$l_e=\min_{h\in D_e}f_e(h)$ and $u_e=\max_{h\in D_e}f_e(h)$.
Reject if $T\notin[\sum l_e,\sum u_e]$, and remove value $h\in D_e$
if $T-f_e(h)\notin[\sum_{g\ne e}l_g,\sum_{g\ne e}u_g]$.
\begin{proposition}
Triangle-support and interval propagation preserve every feasible phase
assignment. Exhaustive branching on the anchor edges, with exact leaf
checks, enumerates each phase-gauged lift once.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every feasible triangle supplies a support to each selected value. The sum
of all other selected edge contributions lies within their summed bounds,
even when those edges are correlated. Thus neither rule deletes a feasible
value. Anchored phases determine all edges uniquely, and branching assigns
each such phase tuple exactly once. Theorem~\ref{thm:projected} and the exact
phase identity give the leaf criterion. A stopped traversal gives only a
lower bound, never an exact count.
\end{proof}

The row-conditioned variant first exhausts the smaller row and stores every
phase tuple in its projected-signature bucket. For a partial assignment of
the other row, all assigned edges contribute exactly; every remaining edge
contributes a rigorous interval. No edge across a partial assignment is
discarded. Besides the $N-1$ projected coordinates, optional folded features
$\PAF(s)+\PAF(N-s)$ have target sum $-4$. For an edge of distance $r$
and weight $w$, its folded contribution is $w$ for differences 0 or 2 and
zero for difference 1, giving a tighter bound than two independent intervals.

For each feature, a sorted index of target values stores cumulative bitsets
of partner signatures. Intersecting the bitsets for the current feasible
intervals removes signatures incompatible with at least one feature; it
does not assert joint feasibility of surviving signatures. An empty
intersection safely prunes the partial assignment. At a full assignment,
exact complementary-key lookup emits every partner tuple in the bucket.
Sound intervals preserve all feasible partners, and the complete stored
row and phase traversal prove exhaustion. Multiplicities are therefore
unchanged. Cycles hold by construction because this variant assigns phases
directly. The precomputed row remains exponential in its active count;
this is a decomposition between the two rows, with no omission of cross
terms within either row and no new complexity bound.

\subsection{Certified spectral rectangles and compact exact joining}
The earlier partner-support search exhausts a row before it can prune the
other row, and reaches its storage cap on the saved $p=7$ branch. We instead
filter each row against the established necessary condition
$\PSD_x(f)\le 2L+2$ for nonzero frequencies \cite{fletcher,lumsden}.
The implementation tests $1\le f\le\lfloor L/2\rfloor$ with $3\nmid f$;
the omitted frequencies are fixed by the length-$N$ compression. Omitting
any necessary test only weakens pruning and cannot remove a valid lift.

Put $S=2^{20}$. Certified integers $c_{fi},s_{fi}$ approximate
$S\cos(2\pi fi/L)$ and $S\sin(2\pi fi/L)$ respectively, with absolute error
at most one. They are constructed without floating point: Machin's identity
$\pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239)$ and alternating rational sums with
32 and 31 terms enclose $\pi$. The interval is rounded outward to denominator
$10^{40}$. Reduction of a cosine argument to $[0,\pi]$, monotonicity, and
alternating cosine partial sums through indices 21 and 20 give lower and
upper bounds. Each stored coefficient is checked to enclose the resulting
interval within its center plus or minus one unit. Sine uses
$\sin\theta=\cos(\pi/2-\theta)$. The coefficient files and generator are
hashed, and the rational arithmetic is independently checked in tests.

For a partial phase assignment $P$, write the approximate Fourier sum as
\[
 \widetilde R_f+\mathrm{i}\widetilde I_f
 =\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{k=0}^2 b_i(c_{f,i+kN}+\mathrm{i}s_{f,i+kN})
 +\sum_{i\text{ active}}d_i(c_{f,i+a_iN}+\mathrm{i}s_{f,i+a_iN}).
\]
Assigned phases give exact integer contributions. For every unassigned
phase, independently minimize and maximize its three possible real and
imaginary contributions. Summing gives integer intervals
$[R_f^-,R_f^+]$ and $[I_f^-,I_f^+]$. Component-wise extrema need not be
simultaneously attainable: the rectangle is a relaxation containing every
actual completion. Inflate each interval by $L$ at both endpoints to
account for coefficient errors in the final binary sum. Let $r_f,t_f$ be
the distances from zero to the two inflated intervals.
\begin{proposition}[Safe spectral exclusion]\label{prop:spectral}
If $r_f^2+t_f^2>S^2(2L+2)$ for any tested frequency, no completion of $P$
can occur in a binary Legendre lift of the intermediate pair.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For any completed binary row, the error in each approximate Fourier
component is at most $\sum_i|x_i|=L$. Its scaled exact Fourier components
therefore lie in the inflated rectangle, so its scaled PSD is at least
$r_f^2+t_f^2$. A Legendre pair has PSD sum $2L+2$ at nonzero frequencies,
and each PSD is nonnegative. The strict inequality contradicts this
necessary condition. Squaring the complete Fourier sum includes every
phase cross term; the rectangle relaxes these interactions without
discarding their contribution from the exact leaf equations.
\end{proof}

The native search fixes each active anchor phase to zero and traverses all
remaining phases in deterministic order. Mode 0 disables spectral tests;
mode 1 tests only complete rows; mode 2 also tests partial assignments.
Deleting a prefix in mode 2 excludes exactly $3^q$ assignments, where $q$
phases remain unassigned. The sum of visited leaves and excluded completion
masses must equal $3^{k-1}$ for every exhausted row. Each surviving row is
encoded as its full negative-support bitmask. All projected PAF coordinates
are evaluated by the exact rotation/popcount identity. A compact hash table
stores an entry for every surviving mask, including repeated PAF signatures.
Hash collisions require equality of the complete integer key; every equal
signature entry is visited. Thus collisions cannot add or omit solutions,
and bucket multiplicities are preserved.

Complementary keys have sum $-2$ in all projected coordinates. Full PAF,
row-sum, compression, and SDS checks validate the emitted masks. Theorem
\ref{thm:projected} supplies the sufficiency argument, and all modes keep
the original phase gauge and the factor-nine ordered multiplicity. Caps
return null exact counts and separately record verified lower bounds.
The native program accepts only intermediate lengths 9, 15, and 21; it
cannot launch a $p=37$ search. This remains exponential row enumeration.
All pruning arithmetic uses signed 64-bit integers. Each coordinate bound
has absolute value at most $3L(S+1)+L$ after inflation, and for $L\le63$
twice its square is below $2^{63}-1$. The threshold also fits; the tested
integer exclusions therefore incur no overflow within the supported domain.

\section{Restricted census and verification methodology}
The catalogue is all admissible intermediate pairs above \eqref{eq:prescribed}
at $p=5$, modulo independent translations by five. Exhaustive intermediate
enumeration gives 10,476 ordered pairs and 1,164 translation representatives.
Its active-count strata have sizes 384 each for $(11,12),(12,11)$; 180 each
for $(10,13),(13,10)$; and 18 each for $(9,14),(14,9)$.
Ranks order these representatives by the existing repository's exact scan-cost
rule and lexicographic tie breaking; the catalogue file fixes that order.

\subsection{A bijective reduction to 79 direct enumerations}
For $p=5$, $\chi_5(-1)=1$. Choose $u\in\Z_{15}^{\times}$, a common
decimation, and independent signs $\epsilon_C,\epsilon_D\in\{\pm1\}$.
Swap the two rows if $\chi_5(u)=-1$, then replace each row by
$C_{\epsilon u i\bmod15}$ and restore its catalogue translation gauge.
The prescription is preserved: the swap compensates for the nonsquare
character, and independent reversals do not alter its character.
Reversal preserves each real PAF, while common decimation permutes shifts.
Every chosen unit modulo 15 is also a unit modulo 45. If the final
catalogue-normalizing offset is $t\in\{0,5,10\}$, the corresponding binary
map is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transfer}
 x'_i=x_{\epsilon u(i+t)\bmod45},
\end{equation}
after the same possible row swap. Compression commutes with this map.
It is a permutation, with an inverse, and preserves the full Legendre
condition. Restoring the phase-zero gauge gives a bijection on translation
classes of binary lifts. Thus exact lift counts transfer, including zero.

Computing this action on the whole catalogue yields 79 disjoint classes:
67 of size 16, 11 of size 8, and one of size 4. Every target retains its own
rank, transformation, count, and solutions; the classes are computational
reuse, not a redefinition of the reported census. No uniform multiplicity
of 16 is assumed. The independent implementation checks every transformed
positive witness and directly re-enumerates pilot targets to check transfer.

\subsection{Two exhaustive enumerators and evidence levels}
The first enumerator uses the ternary Gray update and the 14 projected
PAF shifts. The second constructs binary residue masks by Cartesian products
of negative-support subsets, without using the phase identity, and uses
all 22 nonredundant length-45 PAF shifts. A mask $m$ is evaluated by
$45-2\operatorname{popcount}(m\mathbin{\mathtt{xor}}\operatorname{rot}_s(m))$.
For every directly enumerated representative, the programs compare expected
candidate counts, complete projected-signature histograms including
multiplicities, exact solution counts, and every matching mask pair.
Matched pairs undergo direct integer full-PAF, negative-support SDS, and
compression checks. The intermediate catalogue is regenerated separately.
Known ranks 1 and 2 have 27 ordered lifts each; rank 3 is empty. The
published structured length-45 witness maps to rank 771 and provides another
positive control.

An empty branch is recorded only after both enumerators exhaust their
domains, or via a proved recorded bijection from such a branch. This is a
verified computational nonexistence result conditional on the enumerator
implementations and finite-domain coverage argument. It is not a standalone
PB UNSAT certificate or a formally verified program. The implementations
share the input catalogue and some final-check routines, so independence is
at the enumeration and PAF-evaluation levels, not separate teams or hardware.
SAT proof logs and compact witness certificates from PB runs are checked
with VeriPB. A timeout, cap, or unverified solver status remains unresolved;
it never contributes a zero to an exact census.

\subsection{Pilot, resource gate, and census outcome}
A 21-branch stratified pilot, including known positive and empty cases,
completed in 205.25 seconds on four workers. A conservative direct scan of
all 1,164 branches was estimated at 6,513.69 seconds, exceeding the
repository's 30-minute per-run limit. The proved 79-case reduction gave a

\subsection{Conversion to the published equivalence convention}
For each of the 2,976 canonical pairs, we take a numerical minimum over
common units modulo 45, independent row bracelets (all cyclic shifts and
reversals), and row exchange. Bracelet reduction commutes with subsequent
decimation up to another bracelet operation. Thus the minimum over the 24
units is an exact invariant for the operations in Section 5.4 of
\cite{fletcher}. The mask order used to choose representatives is arbitrary
and is recorded in the code; it need not match the older printed ordering.
The result is 63 classes intersecting our prescribed subset: 61 contain 48
phase-gauged records and two contain 24. Their intersections with the full
prescribed ordered set therefore have sizes 3,888 and 1,944 respectively.
These are intersection sizes, not full unrestricted orbit sizes.
All 63 representatives pass exact full PAF and SDS checks. An independent
tuple implementation checks the canonicalizer on all 1,024 length-five
binary mask pairs and a deterministic sample of length-45 solutions.
The conversion took 1.39 seconds and peaked at 41.46 MB, following a
5.11-second pilot estimate. This computes our labels under the published
convention; it neither reproduces the complete count 3,058 nor matches
individual entries in the historical list.
